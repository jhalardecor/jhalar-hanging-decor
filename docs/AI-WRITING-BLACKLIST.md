# AI writing-pattern blacklist — JHALAR copy

Applies to all new or rewritten copy: site prose, editor defaults, 404/privacy,
meta descriptions, docs written about the brand. Product descriptions in
`content/products.json` are **owner-approved publication records** — do not
silently rewrite them; flag them for review instead.

If a line reads well but you cannot prove the claim behind it, cut the line.

## 1. Banned words and phrases

### AI verbs (replace with a concrete action)
- elevate, elevate your, elevating
- unlock, unleash, embark on
- showcase, show off
- transform, revolutionise, redefine
- craft a (as a verb for non-physical things — "craft a collection" ✗; the
  workshop physically makes pieces, so "made by hand" ✓)
- curate, curated
- dive into, dive deep
- discover (in CTAs — use "View", "Open", "Compare")
- explore (in CTAs — the knowledge base approves "View Collection", not "Explore")
- seamless, seamlessly, effortless, effortlessly
- meticulously, painstakingly, thoughtfully

### AI adjectives (delete or replace with an observable fact)
- vibrant, stunning, breathtaking, exquisite, stunningly
- bespoke, handpicked, premium, luxury (a design brief may say premium; copy
  must earn it with specifics, not the word)
- world-class, unparalleled, one-of-a-kind, unique (unprovable)
- rich, rich tapestry, rich heritage
- stunning, stunningly versatile
- best-in-class, top-notch, high-quality (says nothing measurable)
- "modern and contemporary" (pick one, or describe the thing)

### AI openers
- In today's…
- In the world of…
- Whether you're a decorator or a family…
- Imagine…
- Here's the thing:
- Looking for…? (a rhetorical question as a section opener)
- At JHALAR, we believe…
- We are more than a company…

### AI closers and filler
- We take pride in…
- We are proud to…
- Dedicated to / committed to…
- Your journey (there is no journey; there is an enquiry and a delivery date)
- We look forward to…
- Take it to the next level
- Don't just look — look closer (mimicry of "punchy" copy)
- Say less (and its relatives)

### Inflated claims (forbidden — no evidence, no claim)
- "the finest", "the best", "trusted by hundreds"
- scarcity: "limited stock", "while supplies last"
- authority: "award-winning", "industry-leading"

## 2. Banned sentence patterns

| Pattern | Bad (example) | Good (JHALAR voice) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "Not just X, but Y" | Not just decor, but an experience. | Made for the day the venue has to be ready. |
| Triplet of abstractions | bring colour, depth and celebration | made in our Howrah workshop |
| Triplet of adjectives | full, coordinated and ready | colour, form and finish (concrete product attributes only) |
| "From X to Y, we have Z" | From a single jhalar to full venue installs… | One piece or a full venue — both start with the same message on WhatsApp. |
| Rhetorical question + answer | Wondering how it works? It's simple. | Tell us the design, quantity and date. |
| "The perfect…" | The perfect finishing touch. | A match for the palette you already have. |
| Passive design-speak | is designed to enhance | made to hang at 4 ft and read from 20 ft (only if true) |
| Grand metaphor | colour that speaks | let the colour do the talking (one metaphor per page, max) |

Rules of thumb:
- At most **one triplet per page**, and only of concrete product attributes
  (colour, form, finish, size, quantity, date).
- At most **one metaphor per page**.
- If a sentence has no noun a buyer can touch or count, delete or fix it.

## 3. Banned structures

- Hero = problem → solution → "explore" CTA. JHALAR hero = what it is, in one
  line, then what to do next.
- "Why choose us" bullet lists with bolded lead words.
- FAQ filler (questions no buyer asks, answers that repeat the intro).
- About = "we are passionate / dedicated / proud" self-introduction.
- Paragraphs that open with the brand name and end with the brand name.
- Numbered "journey" steps that describe the website instead of the order
  (the site has no 4-step ordering process; it has one WhatsApp message).

## 4. Punctuation and formatting

- **Em dash**: max one per paragraph in prose. Never in headlines. Product
  names keep their spaced em dash (`Kusum Series — Golden Marigold`) — that is
  an approved naming convention, not copy.
- No ellipses (…) in headings or buttons.
- No ALL-CAPS except eyebrow/kicker labels.
- "and" not "&" in prose (product names keep their existing spelling).
- British/Indian English: colour, catalogue, customise.
- Keep JHALAR capitalised.

## 5. The voice to write in instead

1. Short sentences. The maker's voice, not the marketing voice.
2. Lead with the piece and its use: what it is, what it hangs on, what it is
   for, how many, by when.
3. Concrete nouns only: jhalar, pom pom, gota medallion, bead, bell, toran,
   strand, metre, dozen, venue, pandal, date.
4. One idea per sentence. No clause stacks past two commas.
5. CTA says the actual next step: "View the collection", "Enquire about this
   design", "Start an enquiry", "Message on WhatsApp".
6. If it sounds like it could be on any brand's site, rewrite it until it
   could only be on JHALAR's.

Voice sample (current, approved):
> "Made by hand. Chosen for the moment."
> "Send the design, quantity, location and date. We'll take it from there."
> "Compare colour, form and texture before you enquire."

## 6. Pre-publish self-check

Read the copy aloud. Then:
1. Circle every adjective — is each one observable in the photo or the product?
2. Count em dashes — one per paragraph max?
3. Any banned word from §1? (grep is fine — this list is short on purpose)
4. Does any sentence make a claim we cannot prove? Cut it.
5. Would a decorator in Howrah say this at the counter? If not, rewrite.
